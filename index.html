Sour or bitter? Here's what to change.

This is the heart of Dialed. Tap how your last espresso tasted and see the one change worth making. In the app, the advice uses your grinder's numbers and your own shot history.

Under-extracted

Grind slightly finer

Grind10→9

The water ran through too fast to pull out the sweetness. A finer grind slows the shot down, so it extracts more.

  • Aim for 25–32 seconds at a 1:2 ratio (18 g in, 36 g out).
  • Already slow? Keep the grind and let 3–4 g more into the cup.
  • Light roast? A slightly hotter brew temperature helps.

Over-extracted

Grind slightly coarser

Grind9→10

The shot ran long and pulled out the harsh compounds that come last. A coarser grind lets the water flow faster.

  • Or stop the shot a few grams earlier.
  • Dark roast? Drop the temperature a couple of degrees.
  • Check your beans' roast date: stale coffee tastes flat and bitter.

Too much water

Pull a shorter shot

Yield40g→36g

Strength is mostly about the ratio of coffee to water. Less liquid in the cup, or a slightly higher dose, gives more body.

  • Try 1:1.8 to 1:2 instead of 1:2.5.
  • Or go up half a gram in dose if your basket allows.

Too concentrated

Let a little more into the cup

Yield32g→38g

A longer ratio dilutes the shot and usually brings out more clarity and sweetness, especially with lighter roasts.

  • Try 1:2.2 to 1:2.5.
  • Keep the grind the same so you change one thing at a time.

Uneven extraction

Fix the puck before the grind

Prep✕→WDT

Sour and bitter together usually means channeling: water found an easy path, over-extracting some coffee and skipping the rest.

  • Break up clumps (a WDT tool or a needle), then level and tamp flat.
  • Check the basket isn't overfilled.
  • Then dial the grind as usual.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

How do I dial in espresso?

Keep the dose fixed (for example 18 g), aim for a 1:2 ratio (about 36 g in the cup) in 25 to 32 seconds, then change only the grind. If the shot is sour or runs fast, grind finer. If it's bitter or runs slow, grind coarser. Taste, adjust one step, repeat. Dialed tracks each shot and tells you the next change in your grinder's numbers.

Why does my espresso taste sour?

Sour espresso is usually under-extracted: the water ran through the coffee too fast to pull out the sweetness. Grind finer so the shot runs longer, or let a little more liquid into the cup. Very fresh or very light roasts can also taste sour; a slightly higher brew temperature helps.

Why does my espresso taste bitter?

Bitter, dry or ashy espresso is usually over-extracted: the shot ran too long or the grind is too fine. Grind coarser, or stop the shot a little earlier. Dark roasts are naturally more bitter; a slightly lower temperature can soften them.

What is a good espresso ratio?

A 1:2 ratio is the classic starting point: 18 g of ground coffee for about 36 g of espresso in the cup, in 25 to 32 seconds. Shorter ratios around 1:1.5 (ristretto) taste heavier and more intense; longer ratios around 1:2.5 to 1:3 (lungo) taste lighter and clearer. Try the ratio calculator above.
