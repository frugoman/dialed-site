Espresso guides

Short, practical guides for dialing in espresso. Each one tells you what to change, and why.

Dialing in means finding the grind, dose and yield that make a particular coffee taste good on your machine. The routine is always the same: keep the dose fixed, taste the shot, change one thing, and write down what happened. Sour and bitter tell you which way to go, shot time tells you whether the flow is sensible, and a log keeps you from repeating shots you have already pulled. These guides cover each part in plain language, with the reasoning behind every change, so you can adapt it to your own grinder, machine and beans.

The five-line version

  • Start at 1:2 (for example 18 g in, 36 g out) in about 25 to 30 seconds.
  • Sour or thin: grind finer, or let a little more into the cup.
  • Bitter or dry: grind coarser, or stop the shot earlier.
  • Sour and bitter together: fix distribution and tamp before the grind.
  • Change one thing at a time and write it down.
