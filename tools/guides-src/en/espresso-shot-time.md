The usual answer is **25 to 30 seconds** for a double shot at a 1:2 ratio, for example 18 g of coffee in and 36 g of espresso out. It is a good starting point. It is also the most over-trusted number in espresso, because shot time is a result of your choices, not a goal in itself.

## What shot time actually measures

Time is how long it takes the water to pass through the puck. It depends on:

- the **grind** (finer slows the shot),
- the **dose** relative to the basket,
- the **puck prep** (an uneven puck runs fast),
- the **yield** you stop at (a longer yield takes longer),
- the **pressure and pre-infusion** of your machine, and
- the **age and roast** of the beans.

So the same 28 seconds can taste excellent on one coffee and sour on another. Time tells you about *flow*. Taste tells you about *extraction*. You need both, and when they disagree, taste wins.

## Where to start the timer

Pick one convention and keep it, because otherwise your numbers do not compare. The common choices:

- **When the pump starts.** Simple, and includes pre-infusion. This is the easiest to repeat.
- **When the first drop falls.** Common among people with long pre-infusion; it removes the variable wait.

Neither is more correct. Mixing them is the mistake. A 5 to 8 second pre-infusion makes the same shot look 5 to 8 seconds "slower" under the first convention.

## What shot time can tell you

Used as a diagnostic, time is very useful:

| Time at 1:2 | What it usually suggests | What to try |
|---|---|---|
| Under about 20 s, tastes sour | Grind too coarse, or dose too low | Grind finer |
| Under about 20 s, gushing from one spot | Channeling from poor prep | Fix distribution and tamp first |
| 25 to 30 s, tastes good | You are there | Write it down |
| Over about 35 s, tastes bitter | Grind too fine | Grind coarser |
| Dripping, then sputtering | Choked, or too much dose for the basket | Coarser grind, check dose |

For a gentle change, move the grinder so the time shifts by about 3 to 5 seconds, then taste. See [what to change when espresso is sour or bitter](../espresso-sour-or-bitter/) for the full routine.

## When 25 to 30 seconds is not the right target

Plenty of good shots sit outside that window:

- **Light roasts** are dense and often taste best with a finer grind, a longer shot and sometimes a longer ratio (1:2.3 to 1:3), so 30 to 40 seconds can be correct.
- **Ristretto** (about 1:1.5) usually runs shorter.
- **Turbo shots** use a coarser grind, a larger yield (often about 1:3) and a short time, around 10 to 20 seconds. Fans like the clarity; it is a style choice, not a mistake.
- **Pre-infused or flow-profiled shots** on some machines give very different time-versus-taste curves.

If a shot at 22 seconds tastes sweet and balanced, there is nothing to fix. Do not grind finer to hit a number.

## Time is a better tool when you compare shots over time

A single timing is trivia. A series is information. When you log time next to dose, yield, grind setting and taste, you can see things like:

- the grind setting that gave you your best shots on this bag,
- how much the shot time drifts as the beans age,
- whether a "fast and sour" shot was one-off (prep) or consistent (grind).

## FAQ

### How long should a double espresso shot take?
About 25 to 30 seconds for roughly 18 g in and 36 g out, timed from when the pump starts. Treat it as a starting point: judge by taste, and use the time to see which way to adjust the grind.

### Is a 20 second espresso shot too fast?
It can be. If it tastes sour and thin, grind finer. If it tastes sweet and balanced, it is fine. If it gushes from one spot, the cause is channeling from uneven puck prep, not the grind.

### Is a 40 second shot too slow?
Often, if it tastes bitter or dry: grind coarser. Dense light roasts can taste good at 35 to 40 seconds, so taste before you change anything.

### Should I time from the first drop or from the pump start?
Either works, if you always use the same one. Starting at the pump includes pre-infusion and is easier to repeat.

### Why does my shot time change from day to day?
Dose differences of a gram, puck prep, humidity, the age of the beans and the grinder warming up all change flow. Weigh the dose, prep the same way each time, and expect to nudge the grind slightly as a bag ages.
